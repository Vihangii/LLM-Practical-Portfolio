# Discussion Summary

## How We Worked

We used the LLM one step at a time and discussed each answer as a group before moving to the next step. We did not accept the answers directly. We checked if they made sense for students like us and changed the idea based on our discussion.

## Key Decisions

| Decision | What we decided | Why |
|----------|-----------------|-----|
| Problem | Group 8 - The Budget-Challenged Student | This was the challenge given to our group. It is also a problem we face ourselves, because small daily expenses like canteen food, bus fares and printing add up quickly and it is hard to know where the money went. |
| Product idea | One app that combines quick logging (voice, receipt and bank SMS), automatic categories, a daily "safe to spend" amount, an AI chat and saving challenges | Each of the LLM's ideas solved only one part of the problem. For example, voice logging helps with cash but does not tell the student how much they can spend. Combining the best ideas gave one app that covers recording, understanding and controlling spending. |
| Target user | Sri Lankan undergraduates aged 18-25 living on a monthly allowance | The research showed that these students mostly pay in cash and receive money at irregular times (Mahapola, parents), which normal budgeting apps do not handle. As students ourselves, we understand this user well. |
| Name | SpendSense (our own name, not one from the LLM's list) | The name clearly says what the app does: it helps students make sense of their spending. It is short, easy to remember and easy to understand for everyone. |
| Changes after the critique | Start with a core of quick logging, categories and the safe-to-spend amount, with chat and challenges added next. Use notification access or "share SMS" on Android plus manual entry instead of reading SMS directly. Add voice logging, one-tap repeats and a weekly cash check for cash spending. Keep the safe-to-spend calculation simple and transparent. | The LLM showed that our first idea had too many features for a first version, that Google Play restricts SMS access, that cash spending would still be missed, and that a wrong daily amount would make students lose trust in the app. |

## Points We Noticed in the LLM Output

- The LLM said its problem list was based on known patterns and not on real interviews, so each problem should be treated as something to test with real students.
- The LLM pointed out that Google Play does not allow most apps to read SMS messages, and that iOS does not allow it at all. This is a big limitation for automatic tracking.
- The LLM said that most student spending is still cash, so quick manual logging is still needed even with SMS tracking.


## Group Discussion Points

Our group had two members, so most decisions were made by talking through each LLM answer together.

- **Is automatic SMS tracking realistic?** The LLM showed that Google Play restricts SMS access and iOS does not allow it, so we could not rely only on SMS. This is why quick voice and receipt logging became an important part of the app.
- **How do we handle cash?** Most of our own daily spending is cash (canteen, bus, printing), so the app had to make logging cash faster than opening a normal budgeting app. We chose voice logging in English, Sinhala or Tamil for this.
- **One number instead of many charts.** We agreed that students do not want to read reports every day, so the home screen focuses on one "safe to spend today" amount.
- **Too many features?** The critique said our idea had too many features for a first version. We kept all of them in the mock-up to show the full idea, but the main features are logging, categories and the safe-to-spend amount.



## Conclusion

Working on this activity showed us that an LLM is very useful as a product-development partner. It helped us research the problem, come up with ideas quickly, find weaknesses in our idea and create a realistic user persona. However, we could not use its answers directly. Some suggestions did not fully fit our situation, and the LLM itself said that its problem list was based on assumptions and should be tested with real students. The final decisions, such as combining the ideas into one app, focusing on a single daily amount and choosing the name SpendSense, were made by us. Overall, we learned that LLMs are most useful when they support human thinking instead of replacing it.
